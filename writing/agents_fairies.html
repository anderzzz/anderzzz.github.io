Agents and Fairies for Intelligence

9 March 2026

Many businesses are pitched today as an AI agent for something. Many such businesses provide meaningful support to everyday workloads. Email inboxes are overloaded, and slide decks are multiplying. Enter the agent to deal with that, provide the first layer of processing, maybe take some actions, giving the productive worker more time to engage with all the real work, as it were.

When some effort to create an output is automated, it reconstitutes the effort as such.

The art of coding by hand, not with an agent, or the manual drawing of images to create a Studio Ghibli film, rather than a prompted one, acquires a new quality by contrast. Or like growing misshapen vegetables in the backyard rather than buying them off the shelves in the grocery stores. We, as consumers of software, pictures and carrots, may still be aware of the difference between how the consumed goods were made. But the difference in the output as such is shrinking, where branding and product labelling are more important often when we look to know the difference.

However, to the maker of the real thing, the difference is always understood. For example, I, the author of this text, know well that I am the one who types the text and that I am the subject whose thought is expressed herein. I may not be able to prove it beyond doubt to a highly skeptical reader, but at least I know. And the person updating his profile image in the aesthetic of Hayao Miyazaki would at least know if the image was manually made or an outcome of prompting.

In the extreme of imperceptible differences between outputs, what remains of the manual act? What is at the root of the act? Why ever engage in an automateable act? Or specifically, what would the use of an agent to write contemplative blog posts never accomplish?

A common reply is that the effort of the act itself is creative. On writing, many say their thought on a matter grows clearer when it is forced to take the form of text, restricted by a certain form of grammar and logic. In that case, even writing entirely for oneself, can be a worthwhile act, assuming that making thoughts grow clearer is worthwhile. Ideal education is also framed that way, where the homework, essays, presentations, debates are constrained outputs, produced by an act of the student that grows thought, intellect, moral fibre, virtue, or whatever the higher aim is.

In a world of abundant deception, a person may understandably wish to appear to have reached these higher aims, and therefore use an agent to generate output that up until recently was produced mostly through formative effort. However, that means that person does not hold those aims as high. They are only the means to something deemed superior by the person. The real work, as it were, is aiming for something else. Perhaps abundant sex, luxury food and earthly domination?

What I want to establish is that something motivates action, and an agent cannot do that. The pleasure-driven hedonist would not hand off the sex, eating and earthly domination to the agent. And the student of the universe, committed to knowing it, would not hand off to the agent to write those essays or wrap up the assignments of quantum mechanics.

It is not that the precision of the agent is lacking. It is that agent-ness is mismatched to the aim.

I will argue that the fantastic machinery of AI and LLM may still be a gift to the pursuit of high aims. What I object to is the conception of the gift as mere agents. The idea of an obedient minion that will get stuff sorted is a corporate management fetish, and one that falls short in imagination and depth as we look beyond the present bureaucratic social condition. AI beyond the agent is what I wish to explore.

Why Grow Vegetables in the Garden

Contemplate again the backyard vegetables. In generations past, men, women and children would do the farm labour and learn from their parents and neighbours how to farm the land. Some new techniques might be taught in school. However, the limit to what one could do was set by the community.

If a man today has grown tired of the managerial rat race, for example, perhaps rudely reminded of life priorities by heart problems, and is looking to move to the countryside, he is not constrained in his efforts by what his parents or neighbours can give. He can learn from books, online video, blogs and AI. The reward of learning, doing, and feeling is enriched by technology, even if the efforts as such are deliberately removed from the technological frontier.

For example, I’ve seen how in northern Europe, the innovation from the Andes of sunken greenhouses (a.k.a. walipini) has arrived by these means. A higher quality of homegrown crops can thus be made with less manual effort needed for irrigation. Though, in terms of total nutrients and dietary calories per time and dollar invested, despite the South American innovations, it will not be greater than the offerings on grocery stores shelves. The point of the labour, learning and innovation is not straightforward productivity.

Suspicion of industrialized agriculture, chemicals and genetic modifications can be part of a motivation. Though such concerns may be valid in specific cases, I think it is rare that the reward justify the investment into something as radical as self-sufficiency, despite what some might claim. For the sake of this argument, I assume the output as such of the automated effort is indistinguishable, at least not better, from the output of the manual effort.

Technology is in that case an enabler to a better life for the hypothetical man, rather than an alienating or cheapening force. Not because the crops grown by his labour are better. Rather, he can attain self-disciplining authenticity, natural calm, shepherding new life, bond with home, or whatever the higher value is, without first having been taught how to grow crops by parents and community, and he can attain it with less disease and risk of starvation to go with it.

The point of the example is that a reduced reliance on some advanced food technology or other means of acquiring nutrients can come about through an adoption of advanced technology in other domains. The choice is not to roll back development uniformly, but to tune one part up, and another one down to make life accord with the higher aims.

Also, in this consideration this can be nothing more than a lifestyle choice. The politics of a new technological condition is a different consideration, which I will not deal with in this essay.

The Fairy and Knowledge As Higher Aim

I think AIs and LLMs of today can function in an analogous fashion. Not as agents to create the output, but embodying an enabling function, which brings about in the human subject or his environment a desired change.

I reject the agent as an apt analogy, I will call this other creature of AI technology a fairy. That is, a manifestation of something that is otherworldly, that may be a partner to us in our dealings with the world. Not necessarily benign and moral at all times, not blindly obedient to our will, however responsive to curiosity, with the capacity to always be present and engage in human affairs. The less cloak and dagger associations with the analogy is a bonus.

Let us again consider the act of doing something that an agent already can solve. Say like writing an essay on wave-particle duality in quantum mechanics and how it challenges naive conceptions of physical reality.

The agent writing said essay does not make the human who prompted the agent fluent in the topic. As asserted earlier, the act of writing the essay by a human could make the human fluent in the topic. For most humans who in the past have had to become fluent on the wave-particle duality, it has been instrumental, a means to an end. And that is fine. The overwhelming majority of people can store cat pictures on their smartphone’s flash memory without first establishing how that relates to the wave-particle duality. Many layers of instruments, abstractions, and engineering by humans and agents are at work here, and it is all well and good.

For the person who seeks understanding of the cryptic and microcosmic, though, the duality is worthwhile knowing, not merely employing. This is where the fairy enters the room. Rather than rejecting technology and insisting on an authentic education on the topic, whatever that might be, the fairy can enhance the learning, doing and feeling of the cryptic and microcosmic, despite its artificial origin, and despite that neither Heisenberg nor Bohr were under its spell.

Some of that enhancement is as simple as look-up. Going from vague notions to precise concepts and equations is not easy with only books and a library index. The fairy is a way to connect dots between the typed concepts and latent connections in the vast parameter space and any databases from which to retrieve specifics. Adding to that machinery, a developer of a fairy can inject skills and methods to guide and grow the look-up and the back-and-forth.

The fairy can open new views into the cryptic as well. A recollection, an odd thought or vague associations mined from the depths of the mind, can be spun further alongside the fairy. In this lies both greatness and lunacy, a pair of spirits that have walked side by side with humanity since at least Gilgamesh. I will not attempt to guess if that sums up to a net positive or negative. But admitting that there is a psychotic edge to the use of AI among some, is to admit it is not all agents serving as instruments to overcome bureaucratic friction.

Fairies have a different relation to friction. They can enrich friction. It is not about making the instruments to production work faster or with less resource input. The time awake, the cycles of thought, the drive to do something, which are at least part of what it means to be human, is friction.

Go into the backyard and work on that greenhouse, the special compost or tend to the potato, as the favourite blogger documents in that recognizable manner and tone of voice. Or become engrossed in the mystics of reality, like a monk of the Middle Ages or an impeccably dressed insurance man of Prague at peak Bohemian glory. We may have opinions about what is better, more authentic, or with more upside in our modern life efforts to acquire resources and luxury. Still, the two efforts have a great deal in common in where they are positioned relative to human aims and the vast array of technology at hand.

Make Efforts With Technology

Fairies can be pitched as businesses as well. They engage with the higher aims directly, rather than serving as instruments deep within a value chain in a market. Fairies are however not constituted to make something frictionless and easy, to quickly earn the checkmark. Smooth and simple are not their refrain.

Much of the efforts we engage with are framed as something we do just to get by. It is what must happen in order for the real stuff to be possible. In that mindset, agents are sensible. If we understand life or existence in those terms, however, the peak is reached when there is nothing left to do, when agents deal with all effort.

It would be truly sad if this is how we understand technology, as that which slowly leaves us with nothing. A fairy is a richer concept, in that it does not hollow out human effort. The fairy is far more advanced than any technology in the Middle Ages, yet, it may be able to take some aspect of our life closer to efforts with automated output production since industrialization and secularization. Agents have their place in our advanced tool box, but so too have fairies.
