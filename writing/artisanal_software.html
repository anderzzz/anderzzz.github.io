Artisanal Software is Now

12 June 2026

I have nephews. They wanted to explore sound creation and experimentation. I am the knowledgeable uncle, so I took the task upon myself.

When I was a child, sound exploration meant cassette tapes and the creative distortion of slowing down or speeding up playback. The tapes never left the house. Some silly things are best done in private.

Digital audio tools exist nowadays. But my nephews speak Swedish, so few apps are accessible to them without adult supervision. Also, many apps, for good reasons, send data to cloud servers. But a laptop connected to the internet, sending data back-and-forth to other people's servers, becomes a site of rules and restrictions, hampering free experimentation.

And making something artisanal, even if digital, as a gift carries more significance. That is, created, not with an abstract archetype in mind, but for actual persons.

So, with essential support from AI coding agents, I made an app to this end. MusikBurk, I called it: a fully local, Swedish-language audio synthesizer for the nephews I know. It runs on their computer. It stays there.

The recent advances in AI coding agents made this possible. Not because they can create a feature-rich audio editing app from one quick and easy prompt — that is still hype. Rather, the agents accelerate implementation of logic I know, and fill in the gaps between the different parts.

For example, I am very familiar with digital signal processing, which is at the heart of an audio exploration and editing app. Still, it takes time to type out the code and smoke out the bugs that come along for the ride. Given my feature specifications, the agent creates the code in a matter of minutes.

Then there is the user interface. I have never designed nor implemented a UI before. I have intuitions, though. Until the coding agents attained their current proficiency, my means to express those intuitions were very limited.

Agents are not magic. I have to know what to create, then I ask for it. Also, left unsupervised, agents produce bloatware with impressive consistency: redundant code, contradictory documentation, features that reference other features that don't exist. And at times the agent makes unexamined assumptions, and consequently some drag-and-drop action becomes a bit shaky or a sound transformation is outside meaningful bounds. Only using the app reveals these flaws. There is still friction that knowledge must overcome.

My efforts shed light on a less heralded corner of the world of AI — call it artisanal apps. Not a new thing. In a faraway time I wrote software in Turbo Pascal for my own amusement, like a circle bouncing around on the screen. Now, however, with limited effort, an artisanal app can do more. I doubt this is the domain of billion dollar market caps and headline news. But not everything has to be.

A project that would have taken months now takes weeks. No big user base required to justify the investment. No full-stack team needs convincing. An artisanal project that did not make sense to pursue a few years ago is now an option.

So far the nephews have produced mostly distorted voices, spooky clarinet remixes and chaotic drum beats. Whether this is the beginning of a modernist musical career, a launching point into signal processing studies, or just a bit of childhood fun remains to be seen.
